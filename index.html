    What was on the slide

      Lab notes

      The cone of light

      An objective collects a cone of light from each point of the specimen. Its half-angle θ is set by the numerical aperture, NA = n sin θ. A wider cone resolves finer detail but squeezes the depth of field, which is why 100x is so twitchy.

      House rules of the bench

      01

      Coarse first, at 10x only

      At 100x even a slow 10° nudge of coarse moves about 15 times the depth of field, and a fast spin drives the lens into the slide.

      02

      Parfocal lenses

      Focus carries over when you swing objectives, so 40x and 100x need only a small turn of fine focus.

      03

      Oil at 100x

      The front lens has to sit in the oil. Without it the usable NA drops to about 0.95 and the image stays dim and soft.

      04

      Watch for dust

      The top of the coverslip sits 170 µm above the smear. Specks up there come into focus first.

      How focus is scored

      A

      Graded by eye

      Depth of field is the range where the image already looks sharp. So you're scored on the blur you can actually see on screen: a few microns off at 10x is invisible and costs nothing, while a quarter-micron at 100x shows and counts.

      B

      Real slides have depth

      A smear isn't flat: cells sit at slightly different heights, and thick patches can't all be sharp at once. Each field is measured against the sharpest plane it allows, and the verdict draws that curve with where you locked.
