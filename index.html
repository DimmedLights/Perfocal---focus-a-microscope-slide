A daily microscope game

Focus the slide before the clock runs out.

Find the specimen at low power on the coarse knob, swing in the next objective, then add oil for the last one. Your score is how close you land to the right plane, measured against each objective's real depth of field.

S. aureus · 100x oil

The optics are real

Blur in the eyepiece is computed from defocus distance and each objective's numerical aperture. Higher NA means a wider cone of light, so every micron of error spreads into a bigger blur and the in-focus band gets thinner.

One coarse tick moves the lens 20 µm. One fine tick moves it 0.25 µm.

Coarse first, at 10x only

At 100x a single coarse tick is about 40 times the depth of field, and seven of them can drive the lens into the slide.

Parfocal lenses

Focus carries over between objectives, so 40x and 100x need only a few fine ticks.

Oil at 100x

Without oil the usable NA drops to about 0.95. The image stays dim and never gets crisp.

Watch for dust

The top of the coverslip sits 170 µm above the smear. Specks up there come into focus first.

Three microscopes, three kinds of focus

Brightfield

Light passes through a stained smear. Out-of-focus planes turn into soft blur, and the coverslip dust is the classic trap.

10x/0.25 · 40x/0.65 · 100x/1.25 oil

Fluorescence

Each label glows only through its own filter cube, so you switch cubes to find each structure. The dyes photobleach while the shutter is open.

DAPI · Alexa 488 · phalloidin-568

Confocal

A pinhole rejects out-of-focus light, so other planes go dark instead of blurring. You slice through a thick organoid to find each labelled layer.

405 · 488 · 561 nm lasers · 1 AU pinhole
